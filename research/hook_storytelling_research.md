# Hooks + storytelling for Shorts — research summary (Claude, 27 Nov 2026)

Evidence quality: most hook/retention numbers online come from creator blogs and tool vendors (vidIQ, OpusClip, CapCut...), not from
YouTube or peer-reviewed studies. Solid sources: Loewenstein 1994 (psychology of curiosity), Parker/Stone (but/therefore), Pixar /
Emma Coats (story spine), YouTube's own March 2025 view-count change. Treat every percentage as a rough benchmark, check against our Studio data.

## 1. How Shorts are distributed (practitioner consensus)
- Each Short is tested on a small seed audience; reach grows or stops on whether they stay or swipe. Most swipes happen in the first 1-2 s.
- "Viewed vs swiped away" (VVSA): Paddy Galloway's study of 5,400 Shorts / 33 channels (Twitter thread, ~Apr 2023): Shorts under ~60 % VVSA
  rarely performed; "make the first second really punchy". vidIQ suggests ~70 %+. (Our channel: median 33 % in the last week, 56 % before.)
- Since 31 Mar 2025 a public view counts on every start/replay; the old strict count is "engaged views" (used for YPP / revenue).
  Whether replays raise distribution is NOT confirmed: judge loops by retention, not replay counts.

## 2. Why curiosity works (Loewenstein 1994, Psychological Bulletin 116(1))
- Curiosity = the feeling of a GAP between what you know and what you want to know; it pushes you to close the gap.
- Small, specific gaps make the strongest curiosity; a huge gap (no idea at all) makes little. You need SOME prior knowledge to be curious.
- -> Why game tropes win for us: everyone already knows final bosses / NPCs / tutorials, so "Why do...?" opens a SMALL gap on familiar
  ground. Real-world facts (lighthouse, cabbage) start from zero knowledge = a big gap = weak curiosity. This matches our data.
- The gap must close inside the video (an unpaid promise kills trust and the next video's retention).

## 3. Open loops (practitioner advice)
- Open a question early, answer it late; open the next one when one closes (= our re-hook rule).
- Loops can be VISUAL (a counter, a shadow, a closed gate), not only spoken.
- Do not repeat the same trick ("wait till the end") every video: rotate loop types (question, countdown, mystery object, "what will he do").

## 4. Story structure
- **But / therefore (Trey Parker + Matt Stone, NYU 2011):** beats joined by "and then" are not a story; every beat should be linked by
  BUT (conflict) or THEREFORE (consequence). Test: read the shot list aloud with "but / therefore" between shots; any "and then" = a dead beat.
- **Story spine (Pixar, Emma Coats 2011; from Kenn Adams' improv):** Once upon a time... Every day... One day... Because of that...
  Because of that... Until finally... -> for 30 s: skip "once upon a time / every day" (the trope already says it), START at "one day".
- Coats' list is inspiration, not a formula (Bugaj's critique): use it to check, not to write.

## 5. Loops
- Plan the ending first: the last frame and the audio should flow into frame 1 (same subject position, camera, motion direction, light, sound).
- Loop seams usually break on subject position, angle, motion direction, lighting, background or audio.

## 6. What this changes for us (proposed, not yet in the rules)
1. Add a "but / therefore" check to the self-check: no "and then" between beats.
2. Pick tropes the viewer KNOWS (small gap); avoid real-world facts that need explaining.
3. Target VVSA >= 60 % per video at 48 h; below that, fix the first second before anything else.
4. Rotate loop types across videos; close every gap the video opens.
