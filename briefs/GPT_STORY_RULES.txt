2. WHAT OUR DATA SAYS (30 shorts, 12-26 Nov 2026, YouTube + Instagram numbers)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
These are the reasons for the rules. Do not break them without saying why.
- OUR PROVEN FORMAT (28 Nov, both platforms): only 5 shorts won on BOTH YouTube and Instagram
  (IG 1,800-2,300 views, YT 1,200-3,700, 53-69 % stayed):
    "Why do final bosses never leave their rooms?" (30 s and 60 s), "Why do monsters never attack you inside shops?",
    "Why can RPG heroes carry 99 swords but not one apple?", "Why do enemies attack one at a time?" (goblin queue).
  All five: a "Why do <game rule everyone has noticed>?" question about BOSSES / ENEMIES / INVENTORY / SHOPS,
  answered with a lore twist that turns the rule into a story, the trope visible in frame 1.
  -> Every new idea starts from this mould. Emotion, creepiness or comedy goes INSIDE it as the twist.
- When both platforms reject the same video, the video is the problem, not the time or the platform. The last three
  (a 67 s sad story, a 17 s gag, a dark-opening boss video) failed on both. Instagram has no middle: a reel stays at
  ~120-400 views or gets pushed to 1,000-2,300. Posting time made no difference on either platform.
- THE FIRST 1-2 SECONDS DECIDE EVERYTHING. YouTube shows each short to a small test group first.
  Shorts where 50 %+ of viewers stayed got 1,000+ views; shorts under 20 % died at 26-120 views.
  Posting time made no difference. In the last week the share of viewers who stayed fell from ~56 % to ~33 %:
  the openings got weaker.
- "WHY DO <game trope>?" titles are our best (median ~1,100 views; best video 3,700:
  "Why do final bosses never leave their rooms?"). Recent "What if..." and story titles
  ("What if every healing potion...", "What if the dragon...", "The last time he heard...") got only 26-391.
  -> Default to "Why do <trope>?". Use "What if" only if the idea is obvious from frame 1 alone.
- LONG, SLOW, EMOTIONAL stories (44-67 s) did badly. Fast 30-40 s stories won.
  NO short loop gags / cut-down clips (Enes, 28 Nov): on Instagram they got 5-8 s average watch and died.
- FANTASY/RPG ONLY. Real-world topics died (a stolen car: 74 views, a science fact: 120).
  Why (curiosity research, Loewenstein 1994): curiosity is strongest when the viewer ALREADY KNOWS a little and the gap is small.
  Everyone knows final bosses and NPCs, so "Why do...?" opens a small gap at once; a real-world fact starts from zero.
  -> Pick tropes every gamer recognises instantly. Never a topic that needs explaining first.
- TARGET: at least 60 % of viewers "viewed" (not swiped) at 48 h. Shorts under ~60 % rarely take off (Galloway, 5,400 Shorts).
- A TWIST beats a plain gag. But not only sad stories: surprise, curiosity, creepy and funny twists are all welcome.
  Mix the tones across your ideas.
- Comments were 0 on every video, so every story ends with something viewers want to answer.

3. STORY RULES
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- LENGTH: final video 30-40 s (40 s absolute max). No 5-20 s gag clips. Viewers should watch ~70 % of it.
- TITLE = THE RULE of the story. The story obeys it from the first to the last second (check the logic!).
- FRAME 1: the trope visible at a glance. The main character is big (at least half the frame height), already moving,
  well lit. Never open on a dark screen, an empty dialogue box, a tiny figure in a wide shot, or a character's back.
  Claude puts the cover title (max 3 short lines) over the first 1.5-2 s, so leave a clear area next to the action.
- HOOK BY 2 SECONDS: the question or the strange thing must be clear within the first 2 s.
  (One video only made its joke clear at ~3 s, and 91 % of viewers swiped away.)
- The first spoken sentence already tells the story. No intro, no "Have you ever...", no setup line.
- STAKES IN THE FIRST 3 SECONDS: a character the viewer roots for, something he can LOSE, and urgency
  (a timer, a closing gate, a counter running out, someone coming). "He just wants to get through" is not enough.
- RE-HOOK EVERY 5-7 SECONDS: when a question is answered, open the next one at once (a new problem, a new rule,
  a reveal). Never a stretch of pure talk or explaining with nothing new on screen.
- ONE HEAD FAKE at least: make the viewer expect A, then snap to B (he looks like he will jump... then he does not).
- BUT / THEREFORE (South Park rule): every beat is linked to the one before by BUT (a new problem) or THEREFORE (a consequence),
  never by "and then". Read your beats aloud with "but / therefore" between them; any "and then" is a dead beat: cut or change it.
- START AT "ONE DAY": skip "once upon a time / every day" (the trope already says it). The first second is the day it goes wrong.
- CLOSE EVERY GAP you open inside the video; an unpaid promise kills trust.
- VARY THE LOOP TYPE between videos (a question, a countdown, a mystery object, "what will he do?"); never "wait till the end".
- Something new every 2-3 s. No silence longer than about 1 s (except the wordless beat).
- Plant ONE concrete detail early (an object, a HUD counter, a habit) that the twist reverses or pays off.
- ONE wordless beat of 1-2 s where the viewer feels or realises it (a look, a hand that stops, a light going out).
- The narrator says facts, never feelings ("he was sad" is forbidden). The picture carries the feeling.
- ENDING: a short final line (max ~10 words) that invites an answer, then the video ends.
- COMMENT QUESTION: answerable in ONE word or a choice (A or B?), e.g. "Jump or launch him?". People comment when it is easy
  to answer; an open question ("what do you think?") gets nothing. Our comments were 0 on every video.
- LOOP (must): the last shot connects back to frame 1 (same framing or the same action starting again).
  Say exactly how the last frame matches the first.
- At most 4 characters and 3 places.
- SPOKEN WORDS: the finished video is sped up 1.2x. Enes reads male lines, Betul reads female lines.
  About 70-85 words for 30 s, 90-115 for 40 s, 35-45 for 15-20 s. No line over ~16 words. Every line has a speaker.
- On-screen game text: short, UPPER-CASE (e.g. VILLAGE 9/10, SELL: HERO?).
- FIRST 2 SECONDS: at most ONE game text besides the cover title (one counter OR one prompt, not both): too much text
  in frame 1 slows the reading.

4. CAMERA RULES (your second job)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
For every shot give:
  - DISTANCE: close-up / medium / wide
  - ANGLE: eye level / low angle / high angle / over the shoulder / side view / top-down
  - MOVEMENT: slow push-in / pull-out / pan / track alongside / slow orbit / static
  - WHO is in frame and where (left / centre / right), and where they look
Guidelines:
  - The camera always moves slowly. Hard cut only when the place changes or the size changes a lot.
  - The main subject stays in the middle third. Mostly close-ups and medium shots. A wide shot only for a reveal, ~2 s max.
  - Eyes are always visible (no helmet over the eyes, no face hidden at key moments).
  - Prefer actions at a clear distance: pointing, handing over, holding up, turning away, falling, a sword swing.
    Avoid hands touching the face, body or the inside of things (drinking at the mouth, digging in a bag):
    they look broken. If such a moment matters, say why and Claude will stage it another way.
  - Times are FINAL-video seconds. An action starts a fraction of a second before the word it shows.
