# INSERT DISC 2 — THE LAST RESPAWN
## FINAL PRODUCTION BRIEF — Blender / Claude

**Status:** Approved story direction; production-ready specification.  
**Format:** 9:16, 1080×1920, 24 fps, **58 seconds / 1,392 frames**.  
**Language:** English dialogue and on-screen copy.  
**Genre:** Dark fantasy / action mystery / tragic time loop.  
**Tagline:** Tiny stories from retro games that never existed.

## 1. Core promise

In the first second, a goblin is struck by an arrow and immediately falls back into the street from above. The knight discovers that the city's respawn bell is failing. An army of empty armor seems to be hunting him, but it is actually following its former commander. He sacrifices his last remaining life to restore the bell. The bell restores everyone **to the start of the same fatal cycle**, not to safety. The ending arrow becomes the opening arrow, with matching framing and sound.

**MUST:** This is a complete self-contained story. The audience must understand the physical events without reading tiny environmental text. No explanatory voice-over is required; characters carry the story.

## 2. World and logic — locked for this episode

**Location:** The Bell District, a forgotten underground borough below Hearthmoor, connected to the wider Insert Disc 2 world. Medieval stone alleys feed into a compact clockwork bell chamber. The district has warm furnace-orange lamps, cold blue mist, heavy bronze gears, rope-and-chain machinery, and a massive suspended bell. The bell tower is visible behind the characters whenever the alley opens up.

**The Bell Rule:** When the bell rings, the district restores its residents to their most recent fixed *cycle checkpoint*, not to the instant of death. Their memories are partly retained. The cycle's lethal hazards repeat unless someone changes their actions.

**The Failure:** The bell has stopped. The Goblin has **one remaining emergency respawn**, which is used in the opening. After that, the counter is zero and another death is permanent until the bell is restarted.

**The Hollow Armor:** Residents who exhausted their respawns in earlier failed cycles have left animated armor shells. The shells are not attacking the Knight: they are converging on their original commander, whose soul was used long ago as the bell's power source. The Knight is that commander, now walking as an empty suit powered by the last residual spark of his own soul. Their synchronized kneeling reveals recognition, not mind control or a sudden new identity.

**The Price:** Restarting the bell requires the Knight's final spark. He ceases to exist as an individual. Everyone else is restored to the cycle checkpoint. The Knight is not restored, because the sacrifice removes his identity from the save registry.

**The Tragedy:** The Goblin returns to the same street a moment before the same arrow is fired. He remembers enough to scream. The story does **not** assert that the loop is metaphysically impossible to break; it shows the immediate danger repeating, leaving room for later episodes.

## 3. Cast and voices

1. **THE KNIGHT — MUST.** Silver plated armor, weathered blue cape, a single pale cyan chest emblem, readable helmet silhouette. Heroic but increasingly uneasy. No detailed face required. Voice: natural young adult, grounded, short lines, controlled urgency. The Knight has no visible body inside his armor.
2. **GOBLIN 47 — MUST.** Compact green goblin, large ears, work apron, one red scarf, readable facial states. Fast, anxious, unexpectedly sympathetic. Voice: higher register, breathy, rapid but clearly articulated. The Goblin is the emotional anchor and final loop witness.
3. **THE KEEPER — MUST.** Stocky elderly bell mechanic, brass mask or simple shadowed face, heavy gloves, lantern, distinctive bell-shaped cap. Voice: low, worn, authoritative; not villainous. Knows the cost but cannot operate the bell himself because he is a mechanism-bound attendant, not a registered life.
4. **HOLLOW ARMOR EXTRAS — MUST.** 5–8 visible modular shells, no speech, low animation complexity. Instancing is encouraged. Do not build crowds.

**Voice recording:** The creator voices all three speaking characters. Deliver dialogue as separate, clean WAV stems per character, with no baked reverb. Claude may use timing placeholders for animatics but should not fabricate a final performance or claim recordings exist. The final edit must be synchronized to the supplied recordings. Optional mild spatial reverb for the Keeper and metallic resonance for the Knight; preserve intelligibility.

### Loop engineering — non-negotiable

- Design the opening and ending as **one continuous virtual shot** split across the file boundary. At 00:57.5–00:58.0, the camera position, lens, Goblin screen coordinates, body pose, lighting, background geometry, and incoming arrow trajectory must match 00:00.0.
- Avoid an outro, black frame, fade, logo, end card, or pause at the boundary.
- The **arrow whistle** must continue seamlessly across the boundary; the actual impact occurs immediately after playback restarts.
- The Goblin's last scream can overlap the arrow whistle but must finish before the cut. Do not repeat “Not again!” in a way that creates an awkward double scream; opening line is the exhausted reaction *after* impact, ending line is the anticipatory scream *before* impact.
- Loop preview must be checked by concatenating the exported clip to itself and reviewing at least two cycles.

## 5. Hook priority and visual hierarchy

**MUST:** At 00:00.0 there is already movement and imminent danger. Goblin and Knight fill at least roughly half the frame height; the arrow is large and high-contrast enough to read on a phone. No opening title card, loading screen, narrator preamble, or slow establishing shot.

**MUST:** The first three seconds contain a visible death-like impact, immediate impossible return, and a character reaction. The respawn is stylized and non-graphic; no gore.

**MUST:** Within the first eight seconds, show the stakes through the counter reaching zero and the silent bell. The counter is a brief, legible overlay, not a full-screen menu.

**NICE-TO-HAVE:** A tiny bell-shaped insignia appears on the arrow fletching; it subtly suggests that the repeating hazard is part of the cycle, but must not distract from the main action.

## 6. Visual identity — evolve beyond generic PS1

**Art direction:** *Haunted clockwork diorama*. Preserve PS1-inspired low-poly silhouettes, restrained texture resolution, subtle vertex jitter/dither, and stylized geometry, but elevate the result with intentional cinematic lighting, richer layered depth, bold material separation, and a unique city silhouette. Do **not** imitate a specific existing game's copyrighted assets or interface.

**Palette:** Furnace amber and oxidized bronze against teal-blue shadow; cyan is reserved for respawn energy and the Knight's soul. Goblin's red scarf is a consistent visual anchor.

**Geometry:** Chunky, memorable proportions rather than generic cubes. Armor with asymmetrical shoulders, angular cape, simple hinged jaw or visor tilt; goblin with large triangular ears; Keeper with a broad bell-shaped hat and lantern. Reusable modular stone facades, arches, balconies, cogs, pipes, hanging signs, cables, and lanterns. Avoid complex cloth or fluid simulation.

**World movement:** Two background conveyor gears, one suspended counterweight, swinging chains, vent puffs, flickering lamps, and a handful of distant shadow silhouettes. The environment should feel alive but never compete with the characters. No realistic crowds or costly particle storms.

**Typography:** Replace generic large centered gaming subtitles with a bespoke restrained system: compact, high-contrast, safe-margin subtitles anchored in a consistent lower region, exact spoken words only; highlight one meaningful word at a time in cyan or amber. Dialogue must remain readable without obscuring Goblin or Knight. HUD counters use a separate diegetic-inspired font treatment. Do not show the series logo during the opening.

## 7. Camera and animation

- **MUST:** Camera tracks the Knight and Goblin closely; keep a recognizable character in frame through almost the entire film. Use 4 reusable camera rigs/setups with animated transitions rather than many arbitrary cuts.
- **Setup A:** Close running tracking camera in alley (opening, pursuit, ending loop). 35–45 mm equivalent, gentle handheld feel, avoid nauseating shake.
- **Setup B:** Shoulder-follow through chamber entrance; use character occlusion for the transition.
- **Setup C:** Tight two/three-character staging at gear assembly; small push-ins and orbital movements during revelations.
- **Setup D:** Hero-side close framing for chain pull and sacrifice, then push toward Goblin.
- **MUST:** Strong silhouette and readable gesture beats: recoil, grab, pull, shield, open armor, kneel, reach, resist, pull chain, disappear, turn toward arrow.
- **MUST:** No long still holds; something meaningful changes every ~2–3 seconds. This does not mean random cuts or perpetual camera shaking.
- **NICE-TO-HAVE:** The cyan emblem subtly pulses in sync with the bell machinery before the reveal.

## 8. Audio design and performance

**Voice priority:** Dialogue remains clear above effects. Natural English performances; do not time-stretch speech to fit an arbitrary edit. Record short individual takes for each line, then conform the animatic to the real durations.

**Audio layers:** Footfalls and armor movement, arrow whistle/impact, respawn crack, rhythmic mechanical gears, loose chains, distant market shouts (unintelligible), a low bell-chamber hum, gear failure, one large bell strike, short energy rush. Avoid constant loud music. Optional low drone that drops out just before the bell strike; no dead-air gaps exceeding roughly one second unless the visuals deliver a major revelation.

**Sound perspective:** Alley sounds are narrow and close; bell chamber has a short stone reverb; the bell strike has a long tail that is cut by the hard cycle reset. Preserve the arrow whistle across the loop boundary.

## 9. Asset scope / Blender feasibility

**Reuse existing:** Knight silhouette/armor, goblin base mesh, stone dungeon material library, save/respawn cyan shader, simple armor props if available. These may need rework for the new visual language.

**New and expensive:** Bell District alley facade kit; compact bell chamber; large bell with rigged chain and two gears; Keeper character; hollow armor variants; goblin scarf/apron; arrow and HUD design. Build **one modular alley and one chamber**, not a fully navigable city. The impression of a larger district comes from parallax facades, arches, fog, background silhouettes, and sound.

**Performance-first fallback:** 5 instanced hollow shells instead of 8; static distant geometry; baked lights; low-density volumetrics or fog cards; simple keyframed chain and gear motion; minimal particles. Protect character readability and loop matching before decorative detail.

**MUST:** Use Blender for scene building, animation, lighting, rendering, and compositing. No AI-generated video or image assets. Python scripts may automate procedural asset construction and rendering. Do not require detailed facial animation, realistic fluids, or cloth simulation.

## 10. Production pipeline and QA gates

1. **Blockout:** Graybox alley, chamber, Knight, Goblin, Keeper, shells, arrow. Produce a timing animatic and frame-0 still. Validate camera lock and physical staging.
2. **Hook test:** Render 00:00–00:08 at preview quality. Verify that arrow impact, respawn, counter, and silent bell are readable at phone size with sound off.
3. **Loop test:** Render 00:52–00:58 plus 00:00–00:03 and join. Check frame boundary alignment, arrow path, Goblin pose, and continuous whistle. Fix before final lighting.
4. **Voice pass:** Insert the creator's actual three-character recordings; adjust scene timing within 58 seconds without deleting story logic. Burn subtitles matching every spoken word exactly.
5. **Look development:** Lock a contact sheet of Knight, Goblin, Keeper, alley, chamber, bell, and HUD before committing to final renders.
6. **Final render:** 1080×1920, 24 fps, H.264 MP4 with clean mixed audio. Export dialogue stems, SFX stem, subtitles transcript/SRT, and a short loop-test preview separately.
7. **QA:** Check first-frame legibility; audio intelligibility; no silent dead zones; no clipping/intersections; stable character identity; no tiny illegible world text; subtitle safe margins; no mismatched ending/start frame; total runtime under 60 seconds.

**Approval gate:** Do not invent missing voice recordings or declare the final export complete before rendering and inspecting it. Present blockout/hook/loop previews for creator review first.
